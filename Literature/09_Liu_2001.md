# Design and Simulation of Automated Container Terminal Using AGVs
**Authors:** C.I. Liu, H. Jula, P.A. Ioannou  
**Conference:** European Control Conference (ECC) 2001  
**Methodology:** Macro-level Terminal Design, Queuing Theory, and Simulation Architecture

## 1. The Macro-Design Shift
While previous papers in our syllabus (like Choe 2016 and Grunow 2006) focused strictly on the *micro-level* routing algorithms for AGVs, this paper takes a step back to address the **macro-level** design of the port itself. It answers the fundamental question: *How big does a port need to be, and how fast must the equipment move, to service a modern mega-ship?*

**Thesis Application:** When you code your PettingZoo Python simulation, you cannot just use random numbers for your environment variables. If you set a crane to move 500 containers an hour, the examiner will fail your simulation for being unrealistic. This paper gives you the exact, academically peer-reviewed numbers (based on the Ports of Rotterdam and Long Beach) to hardcode into your simulation's configuration file.

## 2. Hardcoded Simulation Parameters
In Section 2.1, the authors outline 5 core "Design Considerations" that dictate the physics of the terminal:
1. **Ship Size & Turnaround:** The terminal must service 8,000 TEU ships (mega-ships). The desired target turnaround time for a ship this size is **16 hours**.
2. **Intermodal Split:** Containers arrive/leave via two methods: **60% by truck** and **40% by rail**.
3. **Quay Crane (QC) Speed:** A modern QC operates at a maximum of **50 moves per hour** (in single mode) or **42 moves per hour** (in double/combined mode). 
4. **Required Fleet:** To unload a 3,400 container ship in 16 hours using QCs moving at 42 moves/hour, the math dictates you need exactly **5 Quay Cranes** working simultaneously on one berth.

**Thesis Application (Your `config.py`):** You can directly lift these numbers for your simulation! 
```python
# PettingZoo Simulation Parameters (Justified by Liu et al. 2001)
SHIP_CAPACITY_TEU = 8000
TARGET_TURNAROUND_HOURS = 16
QC_MOVES_PER_HOUR = 42
NUM_QUAY_CRANES_PER_BERTH = 5
TRUCK_ARRIVAL_RATIO = 0.60
RAIL_ARRIVAL_RATIO = 0.40
```
If an examiner questions these constants, you cite Liu et al. (2001).

## 4. Terminal Physics and Road Types
![Figure 2: AGV-ACT layout](/literature/liu_2001/figure2.png)

To meet the 22,000 TEU capacity calculated earlier, the authors define the physical terminal as a grid measuring **1,633 x 1,875 ft (70.29 acres)** containing 36 container stacks.
Crucially, they divide the port's road network into two distinct types:
1. **Transit Roads:** High-speed arteries. No loading or unloading is allowed here.
2. **Working Roads:** Local lanes adjacent to the stacks where actual pick-ups and drop-offs occur.

**Thesis Application:** When you code your PettingZoo grid, you should define your nodes/edges with these properties. The MAPPO agent's Action Space should be restricted so it cannot execute a "Drop-off" command if its current coordinate is on a "Transit Road."

## 5. Task Categorization
![Figure 3: Different Tasks assigned to AGVs](/literature/liu_2001/figure3.png)

The authors categorize the AGV routing into three distinct tasks:
- **Task 1:** Quay Crane Buffer $\leftrightarrow$ Gate Buffer (or Storage Yard).
- **Task 2:** Gate Buffer $\leftrightarrow$ Storage Yard.
- **Task 3:** Train Buffer $\leftrightarrow$ Storage Yard.

## 6. Physical Traffic Rules (Collision Avoidance)
In Section 4.1, the authors explicitly define the low-level physical traffic rules required to stop the robots from crashing into each other:
1. **Intersection Collisions:** Resolved using "Modified First Come First Pass" (MFCFP). Whoever gets to the node first gets the right of way. If it's a tie, the right of way is randomly assigned.
2. **Speed Collisions (Rear-ending):** Loaded AGVs move slower than empty AGVs. To prevent empty AGVs from rear-ending loaded ones, they implemented "Low Speed Zones" where empty AGVs are forced to slow down to match the speed of loaded ones.
3. **Following Distance:** A strict inter-vehicle spacing of **45 feet** is maintained to allow emergency braking.

**Thesis Application:** This section pairs perfectly with Grunow (2006). Liu (2001) proves exactly *how* physical collisions are prevented at the low level (MFCFP, Low Speed Zones, 45ft spacing). You can cite this section to prove to the examiner that you understand the low-level physics of AGV traffic, and then immediately cite Grunow (2006) to justify why your MAPPO agent doesn't need to learn these physics (because the RL agent sits at the high-level Dispatcher layer, while the manufacturer's low-level software handles the 45-foot spacing).

## 7. Simulation Physics (Speeds and Equipment)
The authors define the physical speeds of the equipment:
*   **AGV Speed (Empty):** 10 mph.
*   **AGV Speed (Loaded):** 5 mph.
*   **Yard Crane Speed:** 5 mph. (Takes 15s to line up, 45s to load/unload).
*   **Yard Configuration:** Exactly 1 Yard Crane per stack (36 total).

**Thesis Application:** Another hardcoded parameter for your PettingZoo simulation! The velocity vector of your AGV agent must change depending on its state. If `agent.is_loaded == True`, its speed must be cut in half.

## 8. Destination Probabilities (Job Generation)
![Figure 5: Control logic of AGVs for task 1](/literature/liu_2001/figure5.png)

If you look closely at the flowchart in Figure 5, the authors explicitly define the probability distribution for where a container goes after it comes off the ship:
*   **70%** probability ($0.3 < N \le 1.0$): Sent to the **Import Storage Yard**.
*   **18%** probability ($0 \le N \le 0.18$): Sent directly to the **Gate Buffer** (Trucks).
*   **12%** probability ($0.18 < N \le 0.3$): Sent directly to the **Train Buffer**.

**Thesis Application:** When you code the "Environment Step" in your Python simulation, you need to randomly generate jobs for the AGVs. You should use this exact probability distribution (70/18/12) to generate the destinations. If asked, you cite Liu (2001) to prove your simulated workload matches real-world container flows.

## 9. The Classical Baseline Heuristic (Shortest Queue)
In Figures 5 and 6, the control logic explicitly states how an AGV chooses a destination: *"Choose the yard crane with the least number of AGVs in the queue."*

**Thesis Application:** You need a baseline algorithm to compare your MAPPO agent against to prove your Neural Network is actually learning something useful. This flowchart gives you the perfect classical baseline! You can code a simple "Shortest Queue First" heuristic in Python. If your MAPPO agent learns to balance the port faster than the Shortest Queue heuristic, you have successfully proven the superiority of Deep RL.

## 10. The Throughput Saturation Curve (Bottlenecks)
![Figure 7: Throughput of cranes versus the number of AGVs used](/literature/liu_2001/figure7.png)

Figure 7a shows a classic saturation curve. As the authors add more AGVs, the throughput of the Quay Crane increases linearly. However, once they reach about 48 AGVs, the curve completely flattens out at ~42 containers/hour. Why? Because the physical Quay Crane has reached its maximum mechanical limit. Adding a 49th AGV does absolutely nothing because it just ends up waiting in a queue for the crane.

**Thesis Application:** This is a vital sanity check for your Python simulation. In your Results chapter, you should generate this exact graph: **"MAPPO Throughput vs. Number of Agents."** Your graph *must* flatten out at the top. If your graph goes up forever, it means you forgot to code a mechanical speed limit for your Quay Cranes, and your examiner will catch it!

## 11. Performance Evaluation and The "Idle Time" Trap
In Table 4.1, the authors present the results of a 24-hour simulation:
*   **Ship Turnaround:** 16.81 hours (Theoretical max was 16.2 hours).
*   **AGV Idle Rate:** A massive 36.3%.

Why is the AGV idle rate so high? The authors explain that because the ship was emptied in 16.81 hours, there were 7+ hours of dead time waiting for the *next* ship to arrive in the 24-hour cycle. The AGVs were just sitting around doing nothing.

**Thesis Application (Reward Function Design):** This is a massive warning for how you design your RL Reward Function! If you give your MAPPO agent a penalty for being "Idle" during a 24-hour episode, the Neural Network will get confused and perform poorly, because it's being penalized for the 7 hours where there is literally no ship to unload. 
To fix this, either:
1. Terminate the PettingZoo episode the exact second the ship is empty.
2. Only penalize "Idle time" if there are unassigned jobs in the global queue.
